A Comparison Between Autocracy and Constitutional Monarchy

Two forms of government, autocracy and constitutional monarchy, are implemented in areas all over the world. The most prominent examples of autocracy can be seen in Saudi Arabia and Vatican City. Whereas constitutional monarchies are administered in Canada and the United Kingdom. I will be contrasting these two forms of government through the lens of five questions.

How are Laws Made?

Laws in a constitutional monarchy are made depending on the constitution. In many nations with constitutional monarchies, they choose to uphold a democratic constitution in which the people vote for the individuals that will represent them in the lawmaking process. Decisions in a democratic system reflect the will of the majority. The government is limited in its power and must respect people’s rights.

In an autocracy, laws are made by a small group of individuals. These people hold complete power in the state.

Who Wields the Power?

In a constitutional monarchy the power is shared between the king or queen, the constitutional government, legislature and the judiciary. Power shared between different institutions makes for a decentralized division of power. This allows for each institution to check and limit the power of others to keep a balance.

As for an autocracy, power is wielded by a singular entity, be it a group or individual. This entity makes all the decisions for the entirety of the nation.

What are the Freedoms and Rights of the People and How are They Upheld?

The rights and freedoms of the people in the constitutional monarchy is dictated by the constitution. In Canada, for example, the constitution decrees that people have the freedom of religion, thought, peaceful assembly, and association.

Individual and political freedoms are usually suppressed in autocratic regimes. There exists no incentive to give such freedoms to the people because holders of authority don’t rely on public approval.

How are Leaders Held Responsible?

How leaders are held responsible again depends on the state’s constitution. That being said, leaders are generally held to the same standards as all other people in the nation, meaning that they are punished equally under the weight of the law. This is described by Canada, a constitutional monarchy, as the rule of law.

Leaders in an autocracy are above the law and generally can’t be imprisoned due to their actions. Therefore, they are not held responsible. Autocrats control the judiciary, and thus, they can’t try themselves in the court of law.

How are Public Services Provided?

Public services are provided by the executive branch in constitutional monarchies like the UK and Canada. The executive branch is elected by citizens which ensures that the services reflect the needs of the people.

Public goods are provided completely at the whim of the leaders and not at the need of the people. This is because citizens don’t have the power to influence decisions on public services.
