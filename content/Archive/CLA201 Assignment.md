In southern Guizhou, China a new conodont species Idiognathodus praeguizhouensis was discovered. Living between the Moscovian and Kasimovian time periods, this conodont differs from other known species in its genus by being without lobes. The new species is the ancestor of I. guizhounsis, the evolutionary pathway for this claim is supported by 250 samples of conodont elements (microfossils from the organism). The new conodont is described to have “a longer carina, uninterrupted transverse ridges” as well as nodes on the ventral platform, all of which were lost in its transition to I. guizhouensis. The species would also develop protusions on the back of the elements. Wang et. al have proposed the following order of I. guizhouensis descendants beginning with the new species in early Kasimovian, followed by I. guizhouensis later in the era, then I. luodianensis in the Kasimovian-Gzhelian boundary, and ending with I. nashuiensis in the early Gzhelian.

Researchers tested the effectiveness of a Zinc Stannate and magnesium oxide based electrical resistor with the ability to mimic human synapses. The purpose of this device would be to aid in calculations in a machine that replicates a human brain. Varying the resistance of the device emulates three functions of the synapse, the strengthening of connections between ‘neurons’, the weakening of said connections, and the time-varying response. The computer using the zinc stannate and magnesium based device was found to yield greater complexity than the pure zinc stannate device. By connecting each neuron to each other the two devices were trained to be able to recognize a 10x10 pixel image. The zinc stannate and magnesium based device required 12 trials to reach ~100% accuracy while the pure zinc stannate required 24. Under blue light, the device demonstrates light-dependent behavior closely ressembling the following human synapse functions: weighing of potentials from multiple stimuli in quick succession, short-term to long-term memory transition and accelerated relearning. The improvement is due to oxygen ion vacancies occurring on the surface of the device in the single part device, but on the junction between the zinc stannate and magnesium oxide on the two-part device.

Part I: Summary

Researchers tested the effectiveness of a Zn₂SnO₄ (ZTO) based optoelectronic synaptic memristor (OSM) with an MgO bilayer for neuromorphic computing. The resistivity of the OSM can be varied to emulate the two functions: long-term potentiation/depression and spike time-dependent plasticity. Greater nonlinearity was observed in the MgO bilayer synapses (BL) than the single layer synapses (SL), allowing them to have complex interactions. A Hopfield neural network was trained on the neurons to recognize a 10x10 pixel input. The BL device required 12 iterations reach ~100% accuracy while the SL required 24. Under blue light (405 nm) the OSM demonstrated optoelectronic synaptic behavior, that is paired pulse facilitation, short-term to long-term memory transition and accelerated relearning. The improvement is due to oxygen ion vacancies occurring on the surface of the device in the SL, rather than on the MgO-ZTO interface in the BL.

Part II: Word Etymologies

### Optoelectronic Synaptic

This term is used to describe a light-varying synaptic device, used in neuromorphic computing to store data in a way that mimics the human brain. Opt comes from the Greek ὀπτός meaning visible. Electron can be broken down into the root electr- from the Latin electricus meaning ‘of amber’ or ‘amber-like’ and the root ion which is taken from the Greek ἰόν (neuter present participle of ἰέναι) meaning ‘to go’. The word ends with the suffix -ic meaning ‘pertaining to’ from the Latin -ic-us which comes from the Greek -ικ-ός. Synaptic comes as a direct borrowing from the Greek συναπτικός meaning ‘capable of adjusting’ or ‘conjuction’. The word gives the sense of pertaining to some visible amber-like thing going to the conjunction. This is quite far from the contemporary meaning, likely because it’s built from a high-frequency word, electronic, which tend to have their meanings shift over time. It’s hard to say when the term was first used as a syntactic phrase, but optoelectronic and synaptic have been used in the English language since the 1950s and 1890s respectively. Neuromorphic computing began as a field in the 1990s. The term is a syntactic phrase built through derivation and compounding roots and affixes.

### Memristor

This term was coined by Leon Chua in 1971 in his paper Memristor – The missing circuit element where he uses a morphological abbreviation of the words memory and resistor to form the portmanteau memristor. The term memory is partly a borrowing from the French memoire and the Latin memoria. Resistor comes the English root resist which itself comes from the Latin resistere. The suffix -or meaning ‘one who’ or ‘that which’. Together, the parts suggest that it means that which resists with memory. This isn’t far from the following modern definition: An electrical device with a resistance function and a memory function that are both restored after being shut off.

### Neuromorphic Computing

Neuromorphic computing is another syntactic term used to describe the field in which elements of a computer are modeled after the human brain and nervous system. The first word has the root neuro from the Greek νεῦρον meaning ‘nerve’, ‘sinew’, or ‘tendon’; followed by the Greek root μορφόω meaning ‘give shape’ or ‘form to’. Again the English prefix from Greek origins is used to mean ‘pertaining to’. Computing comes from the English term compute which has roots in the French computer and Latin computāre both meaning ‘to calculate’ or ‘to add up to’. The etymons of this phrase gives the sense of pertaining to nerve-like addition. The term was formed morphologically and uses semantic narrowing of the etymons computing and neuro to create meaning.

# Part IV: Reflections

In re-writing Part III: Revised Summary I’ve decided that this article makes great use of its scientific terminology without making it overbearing. Each of the words I chose to analyze would require sentences to break down their meanings which would make for a lengthy journal article. In addition, without those terms are well known in the field, and without them, the article could actually be harder to read for someone who’s more knowledgeable. The term ‘nonlinearity’, however,  was ambiguous enough in the article that I had to include a short explanation of what was meant in both the original and revised summaries. In conclusion, their was a good use of scientific terminology in this article that added to its clarity and understanding.
