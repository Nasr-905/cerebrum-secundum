Engineering Pathways: Insights from Dr. Larry Mo

I interviewed Dr. Larry Mo, an ECE graduate with over 33 years in ultrasound systems and signal processing R&D. I was thrilled to have the opportunity to interview him, as my research also centres around biomedical imaging. In interviewing him, I wanted to gain better insight into where this industry came from, its current state, and where it’s going.

Interview Questions

1. Reflecting on your time in Engineering Science, was there a single "aha" moment or experience that pushed you toward signal processing?
    
2. How has your understanding of signals processing evolved since you started working in the industry—what turned out to be much harder (or easier) than you initially thought?
    
3. Are there any unconventional career moves you’ve made—or seen others make—that students should consider as part of their professional growth?
    
4. Do you see invention as more of a technical problem-solving process, or as a way to creatively challenge assumptions in your field?
    
5. Startups are known for their unpredictability—what’s one moment at Zonare that felt like it could only happen in a startup environment?
    
6. How did undergrad prepare you for the more clinical side of the ultrasound industry?
    

Reflection

Interviewing Dr. Larry Mo provided a great insight into the world of engineering applications. Dr. Mo shared his inspiring journey, beginning with a desire to save lives through engineering. His entry into the ultrasound industry after his PhD was marked by fortunate timing, as the sector expanded due to the entrance of Siemens and General Electric, the latter of which he would join as a physicist.

Dr. Mo highlighted the dual focus required in this field, encompassing both technical innovation and clinical applicability. He mentioned that his understanding of clinical culture and practices is something that can’t be taught and must be experienced; noting that experiences can vary significantly even between culturally similar countries like Canada and the US.

At ComSilica, Dr. Mo pivoted to broadband wireless semiconductor technology, leveraging his expertise in signal processing while gaining new insights into wireless communication. His experience contrasted the agility of startups with the resourcefulness of large corporations. While GE offered the advantage of scale, including cost negotiations and internal mobility, startups excelled in rapid decision-making and innovation.

This conversation has profoundly influenced my perspective on career possibilities. Dr. Mo's journey underscores the importance of adaptability and continuous learning, qualities I aspire to embody. His career aligns with my view of work as a dynamic interplay of innovation, impact, and growth. It also challenges me to remain open to diverse opportunities, whether in established organizations or through new ventures.
